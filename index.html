Each point represents a wildlife observation contributed by a member of the public. Together, these sightings reveal where people encounter wildlife across Australia.

This dot map shows 4,271 observations of the 10 most frequently recorded mammal species in this dataset, ranked by record count. Each species has a distinct colour; use the dropdown to isolate one species. Some coordinates are obscured in the source. Sightings reflect where people record wildlife, rather than a complete survey of animal populations.

Wildlife observations are not evenly distributed. Some places attract far more records than others.

Hotspots may reflect both wildlife presence and where people are more likely to observe and report animals.

Each coloured square is a spatial bin containing the records within its boundaries. Change the bin size to explore broader or finer patterns. Colours show discrete count ranges: 1–4, 5–19, 20–49, 50–199, 200–499 and 500+ observations. Only cells with observations are coloured. Grid sizes are measured in degrees; their physical area varies with latitude. These are record counts, rather than counts of individual animals.
