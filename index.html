01 / Where

Where is Australia’s wildlife being recorded?

Wildlife recording activity is heavily concentrated along Australia’s eastern and south-eastern coasts. The Brisbane–Gold Coast region alone contains 1,275 records (19.7%), while Melbourne forms another visible cluster. Among the 10 selected mammals, Queensland records the most observations (1,922), followed by Victoria and New South Wales. These hotspots reflect both wildlife presence and where citizen scientists are most active; they do not necessarily indicate more animals.

02 / What

Which species dominate the records?

Observation activity is highly uneven between species. Koalas lead with 1,978 records, far ahead of Platypus (679) and Grey-headed Flying-fox (525). Among the top 10 species, Koalas alone account for 45.1% of observations, showing how strongly a single species can dominate the citizen-science record.

03 / When

Have the same species always dominated?

The leading species are remarkably stable: Koala, Platypus and Grey-headed Flying-fox remain first, second and third from 2021–2025. Greater movement occurs among the lower-ranked species, with animals such as the European Rabbit, Quokka and Tasmanian Devil changing positions over time. The longer-term streamgraph also shows recording activity expanding strongly toward 2024, while the relative contribution of individual species changes across years.

04 / Seasons

Does wildlife recording change with the seasons?

Different mammals show distinct seasonal recording patterns. Koala observations peak around October, Platypus in August, Grey-headed Flying-fox in November, while the Spotted-tailed Quoll peaks earlier in April. These differences suggest that when species are recorded depends strongly on season, although the patterns may reflect both animal behaviour and changes in human observation activity.

05 / A seasonal case study

Following the Orange-bellied Parrot through the seasons

Orange-bellied Parrot records show a striking seasonal geographic shift. In summer, 71.4% of records are from Tasmania, but by winter Tasmania contributes only a small share, while Victoria accounts for 64.2% and South Australia for 28.5%. In spring, Victoria remains dominant at 60.8%, while Tasmania’s share rises again to 26.0%.

06 / Connections

Different species tell different geographic stories

The five most-recorded mammals have very different geographic footprints. Queensland receives the largest overall flow with 1,918 observations, followed by Victoria (735) and New South Wales (571). Koala records dominate the Queensland flow, while other species are distributed more broadly across multiple states, showing that Australia’s citizen-science record varies substantially by both species and location.

07 / Diversity

What does the citizen-science record contain?

The taxonomic structure of the selected mammals is also uneven. Marsupials dominate with 2,768 of 4,271 observations, compared with 824 placental mammal and 679 monotreme observations. Much of this dominance is driven by the large number of Koala records, showing how individual species can strongly influence the apparent composition of citizen-science datasets.
